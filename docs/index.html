The short answer · after the registered v3 tests

What ANM brings TEDDY now

Not better decisions. The response tools gave one validated measurement about TEDDY (E5-M), and it does not settle the registered question. All seven registered tests (v3) are final and give this honest list; the four v2 findings below are kept as a record, each marked where v3 supersedes it.

  1. A check that the declared readouts are what the engine computes. ANM’s engine equals the declared rule (E1.1a: 0 mismatches on site4; E6: 0 in every external donor); its leave-one-out equals the exact closed form (E1.3a: agreement 1.0000, after A1.3 found that the registered form re-coded zeroing an event, while ANM deletes the event’s site); its fusion equals its closed form (E4). Bookkeeping, not news about TEDDY.
  2. No better decisions, by construction here. All events enter at t = 0, so ANM’s decision-layer calls equal a re-coded declared rule. Its readouts (top action score, closure readout, contradiction fusion) did not beat TEDDY’s margin, the mean rule, a trained classifier or a validation-trained stacker at any registered primary endpoint (E1, E4). On the external data TEDDY’s margin ranked the rule’s calls better than the top score in all 8 donors (E6: −0.0087 [−0.0182, −0.0017]; still inconclusive under E1’s own decision rule). ANM’s fusion did beat channel 1 alone on the E4 key (+0.0177), but so does the declared trust-weighted average (+0.0143), and the difference between the two is inconclusive.
  3. One validated measurement about TEDDY, and it does not settle the registered question. E2’s decomposition added nothing beyond the accuracy curve; E5’s registered derivative gate failed in float32. E5-M ran ANM’s sufficiency machinery on TEDDY end to end (derivative gate passed, positive control passed): for E3’s attention-pooling readout, at first order, 0.085 of its response on NK–T look-alike cells flows through what gene-mean pooling discards, against 0.127 on random cells. The registered verdict is inconclusive, so neither “pooling loses the look-alike difference” nor “the gene-mean state is sufficient” is established, and it holds for this one observer only. E5-M →
  4. What TEDDY users can take away now (not ANM-specific): measured operating points under a five-class key with an explicit validity check, which flagged donor 13272 on site4 and the key on the external data (kappa 0.6562); a trained classifier on the same evidence beats the declared rule even for a changed question, though its call-ordering advantage did not carry over to the external data; ranking the rule’s calls by TEDDY’s own margin, ahead in 8 of 8 external donors (inconclusive on site4 and under E1’s own decision rule); a layer-mean MLP or attention pooling over frozen TEDDY did not improve NK-vs-T accuracy over our head.

The principle: a declared candidate retained state is sufficient for a declared readout only if the same state always gives the same readout. Here the candidate is TEDDY’s embedding and the readout the proteins our head predicts from it, so this probes TEDDY’s embedding + our head together. Declaring the state does not establish that it is sufficient.

  • Right direction, compressed. NK has the higher predicted CD56 in 68% of pairs, but predicted gaps are a quarter to a third of measured ones (CD335 0.88).
  • Not generic shrinkage? (v2 reading) Non-neighbour NK–T pairs keep 0.8–1.5, so v2 tied the loss to near-merged cells. v3 E3: on registered pairs the flagged-vs-unflagged contrast is descriptive only and not robust.
  • Partly in our head’s readout? (v2 Mode A probes) A linear probe tells NK from T at every TEDDY layer (held-out AUC 0.991–0.997). On these pairs our head keeps 0.24 / 0.31 / 0.88 / 0.27 of the measured CD56 / CD94 / CD335 / CD3 gap and a linear probe on the embedding about the same (less for CD335), but an MLP probe on the same embedding keeps about twice as much: 0.47 / 0.59 / 0.74 / 0.61 (MLP − head interval above 0 for CD56, CD94 and CD3 at every depth). v3 E3: two trained readouts of frozen TEDDY lost NK-vs-T accuracy to our head.
  • Partly mean pooling, partly not in the RNA? (v2 reading) The states of 13 NK/T gene tokens keep more than the gene-mean of the same forward pass (layer 12, MLP: 0.58 / 0.62 / 0.72 / 0.56 vs 0.41 / 0.43 / 0.49 / 0.45), about as much already at the input layer: it is mostly which genes are detected and at what rank, and TEDDY’s layers neither add nor remove much of it. Even the best probe keeps only about half to three-quarters, and CD3E is among the tokens in only 45% of NK and 60% of T pair cells. v2 concluded that a non-linear, token-aware readout of frozen TEDDY could recover part of the NK–T difference; v3 E3 rejected that (attention pooling over gene tokens, R2, lost NK-vs-T accuracy to our head: −0.1925). Caveats: one site; probes, not the head’s objective; token probes fit on 990 random site4 NK/T cells and scored on the 483 pair cells; some intervals overlap.

00 — The setup

How ANM connects to TEDDY

Three roles, one direction: TEDDY + our head turn RNA into evidence, ANM decides, the measured proteins grade. Nothing flows back into TEDDY.

Evidence source · frozen

TEDDY + our head

  • Frozen TEDDY reads only RNA → a 512-number embedding; never retrained or opened.
  • Our head, trained once on training sites’ proteins, predicts 134 (Pearson ≈ 0.60); v2 used 9 picked by test-set Pearson, v3 uses 12 picked on the validation donor.
  • Embedding from TEDDY-G’s official preprocessing; the earlier non-official one gave the same Pearson.
Decision layer · added, not trained

ANM

  • Reads the predicted panel proteins as typed evidence (v2: 9; v3: 12; exceptions: table below).
  • A written question (the observer) → a call or “no call”: the fixed rule’s call for that question.
  • No learned weights; open loop, so its dynamics are not tested here.
Answer key · held out

Measured proteins

  • CITE-seq measures the same cell’s proteins, kept aside.
  • They grade calls with the rule’s and ANM’s scoring function.
  • v2 key: 3 classes, no NK class (1,342 of 1,690 NK cells counted as “myeloid”). v3 key: B, T, NK, myeloid and out of scope, kept only where annotation and a protein gate agree.

What ANM is for, in the paper

In ANM v2 the retained state is a declared candidate, tested against a declared readout (“Its choice does not establish sufficiency”). The paper’s AI workflows are open-loop with memory (retention, multi-step propagation, histories) and no feedback; there ANM is a response-theory verifier, not a better decision-maker.

Here every event enters at t = 0, so the field acts only as a constant gain per class (G(3)·3·S) and ANM’s decision-layer calls equal a re-coded rule. The response tools were used in E2 (push the RNA), E5 (follow a push through TEDDY’s layers) and E5-M (test the gene-mean as a declared candidate state). E2 and E5 produced no validated finding about TEDDY. E5-M produced one validated measurement for one observer, and its registered verdict is inconclusive.

Overall: in this project ANM worked as a verifier of declared readouts and as a precise way to pose the sufficiency question, not as a better decision-maker.

02 — How we compare

One fair test, reused in every experiment

“ANM helps TEDDY” means TEDDY + ANM beats TEDDY + fixed rule on a decision property a trained head needs labels for, TEDDY and our head unchanged. After the rerun no experiment shows better calls; what remains is a declared layer (written question, checks, per-marker reasons), which a rule written down the same way also gives. v3 kept this definition and registered it; under it, no registered test found help (v3).

Four rules

R1
Same frozen TEDDY and headNeither is retrained (best.pt is the head). Rule and ANM read the same 9 predicted proteins as typed evidence; classifiers in Experiments 1 and 5 add 32 embedding numbers.
R2
Same cells16,750 held-out cells from site4 plus an “out-of-site check” on 2,000; donor 15078 (32.6% of site4) is in training at other sites; donor 18303 was the validation set.
R3
Truth only checks answersMeasured proteins score the calls, with labelled exceptions (withdrawn Experiments 2 and 6); one train-median size factor, 0.92663, not a per-cell one.
R4
Decision metrics firstDeclining, label cost, per-marker reasons, coverage vs accuracy, false agreement. Accuracy is reported, never the win condition.

Three methods

Baseline

TEDDY + fixed rule

Predicted proteins → a score per lineage → the best if it clears the bar (the soft rule).

Treatment

TEDDY + ANM

The same predictions → ANM’s engine under a written observer → a call or “no call”, with workability and exactness checks.

Control

A classifier fitted on labelled cells (inputs vary by experiment), modelled on Jev’s interface; Jev was never called.
