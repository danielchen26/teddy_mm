Keep TEDDY frozen.
Make its answers editable, honest and auditable with ANM.

TEDDY reads a cell’s RNA and predicts its surface proteins (phase-1 Pearson ≈ 0.60). Read by its fixed rule, it can’t change the question without re-coding or new labels, explain a call, or say which calls to trust. We add ANM on top of TEDDY’s unchanged predictions and test each of these head-to-head. After the corrected rerun, with all markers entering at once, ANM makes the same call as the re-coded fixed rule on every cell by construction: its value is a declared, auditable decision layer (a written question, checks, per-marker reasons), not better calls. teddy_mm runs ANM’s open-loop decision layer: TEDDY is a prescribed evidence source and does not read the field, so ANM’s dynamics (state feedback, operator memory, delays) are not tested here.

Corrections (29 Sep 2026): rerun done for Experiments 1, 3, 4 and 5

  • Marker order: fixed. ANM’s engine entered the markers one per step in panel order, a hidden weight. In the rerun all markers enter at once, and the answer key, the fixed rule and ANM share one scoring function. ANM’s scores are then a fixed multiple of the rule’s, so ANM makes the same call as TEDDY + fixed rule (re-coded for each question) on every site4 cell, for all three questions. What ANM adds is a declared, auditable layer: the question is written down, the checks come with it, and every call has a per-marker reason.
  • Per-cell scaling: fixed. TEDDY’s predictions were scaled by a per-cell factor computed from the cell’s measured protein, the answer key. The rerun uses one train-median factor, 0.92663 (67,405 training cells).
  • Which change moved which number. The fixed rule’s “no calls” under the soft and B/T-priority rules (101 → 448, 1,796 → 3,169) moved because of the new size factor alone; the shared scoring leaves them unchanged. The strict rule is redefined (a weighted mean with key markers at weight 2): its “no calls” go 881 → 1,277 from the scoring and 1,277 → 2,014 from the size factor.
  • The old ANM accuracy edge came only from declining more. Old run, B/T-priority rule: ANM 0.960 vs rule 0.943 on calls made, but at ANM’s coverage (3,373 declined) the old rule scored 0.966.
  • Experiment 3: CD5 decides 23.6% → 19.5% of calls; the shuffle now keeps values within their lineage (p ≈ 0.0099, the smallest 100 shuffles can give). With all markers at once the deciding marker is the protein with the largest normalised value in the called lineage, so it is not specific to ANM.
  • Experiment 4: ANM’s confidence (soft_P) gates no better than TEDDY’s own margin at the same coverage (B/T-priority, 40%: 0.9981 vs 0.9985). soft_P reaches 1.11, so it is a score, not a probability. The hard cells are mostly out of scope: NK/ILC 3.4× and erythroid 3.7× over-represented.
  • Experiment 5: labels are now drawn from a pool disjoint from the scored cells (11,725 / 5,025). Labels to match ANM: logistic 500, MLP 2,000 (was 200), threshold grid 50.
  • Answer key. It still has three classes (B, T, myeloid) and no NK or out-of-scope class. Out-of-scope cells (NK, ILC, erythroid, progenitor) still get a call 64–91% of the time, from ANM and the rule alike.
  • Experiment 2 (RNA missing): withdrawn pending redesign. Prototype: with degraded RNA fed to TEDDY, ANM with a declared trust per level makes the same decisions as the fixed rule with a stricter bar per level.
  • Experiment 6 (look-alike cells): withdrawn pending redesign. Prototype: TEDDY does separate NK from T in the right direction but compresses the differences to about a quarter to a half of the measured ones (CD335 about two-thirds). On site4, 918 of the 1,279 NK–T look-alike pairs involve “gdT CD158b+” cells, which TEDDY and measured protein both call NK-like (an annotation problem, not a TEDDY failure); on the other 361 pairs the median within-pair gap in TEDDY-predicted CD56 / CD94 / CD335 / CD3 is 0.176 / 0.137 / 0.248 / 0.195 vs measured 0.563 / 0.301 / 0.360 / 0.738.
  • Phase 1 re-evaluated with the train-median factor: test Pearson 0.610 → 0.603 (MLP) and 0.596 → 0.588 (flow matching); panel proteins drop by at most 0.013.
  • TEDDY preprocessing deviates from the official pipeline. teddy_mm’s embedding is a mean over real tokens at 512 tokens with no gene-median normalisation. Official TEDDY-G returns token 0 by default; its tutorial mean-pools over 2,048 positions after gene-median normalisation. With the medians applied, the cosine to the stored embedding is 0.649.
  • “0 labels for a new question” holds for any written rule, including TEDDY’s fixed rule; it is not specific to ANM.
  • Cells. Donor 15078 is in training at other sites and is 32.6% of the site4 test cells. The “out-of-site check” is held-out donor 18303 at a training site, which was also the validation set. The 9 panel proteins were chosen by test-set Pearson.

00 — The setup

How ANM connects to TEDDY

Three roles, one direction. TEDDY turns RNA into evidence, ANM decides from that evidence, and the measured proteins grade the decisions. Nothing flows back into TEDDY.

Evidence source · frozen

TEDDY

  • Reads one thing: the cell’s RNA.
  • TEDDY-G 70M turns it into a 512-number embedding. A small head trained in this repo on those embeddings predicts the cell’s surface proteins (134, Pearson ≈ 0.60); we use 9, chosen by their test-set Pearson.
  • Preprocessing deviates from the official pipeline: mean over real tokens, 512 tokens, no gene-median normalisation.
  • Never retrained, never opened up.
Decision layer · added, not trained

ANM

  • Normally reads only TEDDY’s outputs: the 9 predictions as typed evidence. Labelled exceptions, both in withdrawn experiments: the protein-only stand-in when RNA is missing (Experiment 2) and its average with TEDDY in “both”; measured protein in Experiment 6’s complement test. Experiment 6’s look-alike pairs are picked by a separate step outside ANM.
  • Applies a written-down question, the observer: marker weights and a bar. It returns a lineage call or “no call”, with checks attached. All markers enter at once, so its call equals the fixed rule’s under the same question.
  • No learned weights: the question and the engine’s settings are a few declared numbers. Open loop: TEDDY does not read ANM’s field, so ANM’s dynamics are not tested here.
Answer key · held out

Measured proteins

  • CITE-seq also measures the proteins of the same cell. They are kept aside.
  • They grade each call, keyed per question with the same scoring function the rule and ANM use: soft averages each lineage’s 3 markers; strict weights the key marker 2 to 1; B/T-priority uses the weighted key marker. A cell counts only when its top lineage clearly leads (by that rule’s margin).
  • The key has three classes (B, T, myeloid) and no NK or out-of-scope class, so most NK cells, which carry CD16, count as “myeloid” (1,342 of 1,690 under the soft rule).

Why two words. Phases are steps in building the model that supplies the evidence: phase 2 was meant to extend phase 1. Modes are not steps but two alternative ways for ANM to engage with TEDDY: on its outputs (B) or inside it (A). The axes are independent. Even unfinished, phase 2 fed Mode B as the RNA-missing stand-in (Experiment 2, now withdrawn). A finished fusion model could be used in Mode B as a stronger second evidence channel that ANM weighs against TEDDY’s; that would still be Mode B, not Mode A.

Is this a multimodal model?

TEDDY is not: it reads only RNA, and nothing fuses protein into it. The data are multimodal (RNA and protein measured in the same cell), and normally the protein is only the answer key. It also reaches the evidence in labelled places, both in experiments now withdrawn: in Experiment 2, with RNA missing, this repo’s phase-2 model (trained on TEDDY’s embedding plus protein, run here with its RNA input off) stands in, and the “both” condition averages TEDDY’s predictions with it; Experiment 6 uses measured protein to pick look-alike pairs and, in its complement test, as ANM’s evidence.

01 — The problem

TEDDY gives good evidence. Deciding from it is the gap.

A frozen RNA → protein map answers one fixed question, the same way, for every cell. Six things a biologist needs from a readout are missing. Each one became an experiment with its own test.

02 — How we compare

One fair test, reused in every experiment

Every experiment runs the same three methods on the same frozen predictions (labelled exceptions in R1 and R3: Experiments 2 and 6, both withdrawn). “ANM helps TEDDY” means TEDDY + ANM beats TEDDY + fixed rule on a decision property with TEDDY unchanged, and a trained head can’t get there without buying labels. After the rerun no experiment shows better calls: with all markers entering at once, ANM’s calls equal the re-coded rule’s on every cell. What remains is a declared, auditable layer: the question written down, workability and exactness checks, and a per-marker reason for every call.

Four rules

R1
Same frozen TEDDYbest.pt is never retrained. TEDDY + fixed rule and ANM read the same 9 predicted proteins per cell, the typed evidence; the trained classifiers in Experiments 1 and 5 also get the first 32 numbers of TEDDY’s embedding. The 9 proteins were chosen by test-set Pearson. Exception (withdrawn): without RNA TEDDY can’t run, so a protein-only stand-in supplied the evidence in Experiment 2’s RNA-missing condition, and its “both” condition averaged TEDDY’s predictions with it.
R2
Same cells16,750 held-out cells from site4, plus an “out-of-site check” on 2,000 more. Not donor-independent: donor 15078 is in training at other sites and is 32.6% of the site4 test cells. The “out-of-site check” is held-out donor 18303 at a training site, which was also the validation set.
R3
Truth only checks answersThe measured proteins score the calls. Labelled exceptions, both withdrawn, also fed them into the evidence: Experiment 2’s RNA-missing stand-in reconstructs the panel from them (half of “both” too), and Experiment 6’s complement test uses them directly. Corrected: TEDDY’s predictions were first scaled by a per-cell factor computed from measured protein; the rerun uses one train-median factor, 0.92663.
R4
Decision metrics firstDeclining when unsure, label cost, per-marker reasons, coverage vs accuracy, false agreement. Accuracy is reported, never the win condition.

Three methods

Baseline

TEDDY + fixed rule

Predicted proteins → a score per lineage → pick the best one if it clears a threshold (the soft rule). What TEDDY gives out of the box.

Treatment

TEDDY + ANM

The same predictions as typed evidence → ANM’s decision engine under a written-down question (the observer) → a call or a “no call”, with workability and exactness checks.

Control

TEDDY + trained classifier

The “just train harder” control: a classifier fitted on labelled cells. In Experiments 1 and 5 it reads TEDDY’s 9 predictions plus 32 embedding numbers; in Experiment 4 (and the withdrawn Experiment 2), the 9 values only. It is a stand-in modelled on Jev’s interface; TypeSafe’s Jev was never called.

help = (TEDDY + ANM) − (TEDDY + fixed rule) on the same predictions,  with TEDDY + trained classifier as the “just train harder” control

So an experiment only counts if the difference comes from the decision layer. Within an experiment, TEDDY + fixed rule and ANM read the same evidence (except in Experiment 6, where the evidence itself is what changes), so their difference comes from the decision layer. In Experiments 1 and 5 the trained classifiers also see 32 embedding numbers, which can only favour them.

How each method does on all six questions is summarised up front in 00 · The setup.

04 — Claim boundary

      
What we claim, and what we don’t

      
This is Mode B: we treat TEDDY as an evidence source and study the decision layer on top. teddy_mm runs ANM’s open-loop decision layer: TEDDY is a prescribed evidence source and does not read the field, so ANM’s dynamics (state feedback, operator memory, delays) are not tested here. Mode A, studying TEDDY’s internals, is not attempted.

      

        
Claimed · the decision layer
    
          
  • TEDDY’s predictions as typed evidence → ANM decisions
    • 
          
  • A declared, auditable layer: with all markers entering at once ANM makes the same calls as the fixed rule written for the same question, and adds the written question, workability and exactness checks, and a per-marker reason for every call
    • 
          
  • Questions (observers) that can be rewritten without retraining TEDDY: soft, strict, B/T-priority (as any written rule can)
    • 
          
  • Per-marker reasons (leave-one-out, flip distance) on the rule that decided
    • 
          
  • Confidence cut-offs, and zero-label adoption of a new question vs a trained head
    • 
        

        
Not claimed
    
          
  • Better calls than TEDDY + fixed rule: the calls are identical by construction
    • 
          
  • A confidence gate only ANM has, or a better one (TEDDY’s own margin gates calls as well)
    • 
          
  • Experiments 2 (RNA missing) and 6 (look-alike cells): withdrawn pending redesign
    • 
          
  • Studying TEDDY’s internals: residual stream, layer Jacobians, in-silico gene perturbations
    • 
          
  • ANM’s dynamics: state feedback, operator memory, delays (the loop here is open: TEDDY does not read the field)
    • 
          
  • The loop as TEDDY’s representation responding to perturbations · gated ±ε nudges
    • 
          
  • Pearson above phase-1 ≈ 0.60
    • 
          
  • Fusion audit · ATAC / chromatin · clinical superiority
    • 
          
  • Anything about TypeSafe AI’s Jev itself: it was never called here (TEDDY + trained classifier is a stand-in modelled on Jev’s interface)
    • 
        

      

      

        
Honest weak spots

        
    
          
  • ANM and the fixed rule make the same call on every cell once both are written for the same question. The first run’s differences came from a hidden weight (markers entering one per step) and from ANM declining more; at ANM’s coverage the old rule was as accurate or more (B/T-priority 0.966 vs 0.960).
    • 
          
  • The answer key has three classes and no NK or out-of-scope class. Out-of-scope cells (NK, ILC, erythroid, progenitor) still get a call 64–91% of the time, from ANM and the rule alike, and they make up most of Experiment 4’s hard cells.
    • 
          
  • Accuracy is identical in Experiment 1. HARD_PROOF_V2’s three-method table lists ANM’s exactness, which counts a decline as a miss (0.938 / 0.927 / 0.835), next to the rule’s accuracy of calls made. This page compares accuracy of calls made for every method.
    • 
          
  • Experiment 4 trades coverage for accuracy, and TEDDY’s own margin gates as well as ANM’s confidence; label-budget gains are small.
    • 
          
  • TEDDY preprocessing deviates from the official TEDDY-G pipeline. teddy_mm’s embedding is a mean over real tokens at 512 tokens, with no gene-median normalisation. The official model returns token 0 by default (no CLS token is added, so it is the top-ranked gene), and the official tutorial mean-pools over all 2,048 positions after gene-median normalisation. With the medians applied (512 tokens, same mean), the cosine to the stored embedding is 0.649 (median over 48 site4 cells). Every result here uses the stored embedding.
    • 
          
  • Phase 1 was re-evaluated with the train-median size factor: test Pearson 0.603 (MLP) and 0.588 (flow matching). Mean R² is low next to those Pearson values (0.111 and 0.046), possibly a scale mismatch between predictions and measurements; not investigated.
    • 
          
  • Donor 15078 is in training at other sites and is 32.6% of the site4 test cells. The “out-of-site check” is held-out donor 18303 at a training site, also the validation set. The 9 panel proteins were chosen by test-set Pearson.
    • 
        

      

      
TEDDY’s embedding (z_512) is the mean of its last-layer tokens over real (unpadded) tokens at context length 512, with no gene-median normalisation (not pretrain 2048, not token 0, not a disease token; recomputing stored embeddings gives cosine 1.000000 at 512 vs median 0.970 at 1024), L2-normalized 512-D (z_rna_512.npy). This deviates from the official TEDDY-G pipeline (see above). The compact z_rna_export.npy (z_keep=32) is not the probe space. Full scope: MODE_B_SCOPE.md.
