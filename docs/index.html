After the fixes · the short answer

What ANM brings TEDDY now

Not higher accuracy. Four things, most useful first.

★ Most useful · the sufficiency test

NK and T cells nearly merge in TEDDY’s embedding, and the protein differences predicted from it are squeezed

The principle: the same retained state must give the same readout; here the state is TEDDY’s embedding and the readout the proteins our head predicts from it, so this tests TEDDY’s embedding + our head together.

  • Right direction, compressed. NK has the higher predicted CD56 in 68% of pairs, but predicted gaps are a quarter to a third of measured ones (CD335 0.88).
  • Not generic shrinkage. Non-neighbour NK–T pairs keep 0.8–1.5, so the loss is tied to near-merged cells.
  • Partly in our head’s readout (Mode A). A linear probe tells NK from T at every TEDDY layer (held-out AUC 0.991–0.997). On these pairs our head keeps 0.24 / 0.31 / 0.88 / 0.27 of the measured CD56 / CD94 / CD335 / CD3 gap and a linear probe on the embedding about the same (less for CD335), but an MLP probe on the same embedding keeps about twice as much: 0.47 / 0.59 / 0.74 / 0.61 (MLP − head interval above 0 for CD56, CD94 and CD3 at every depth).
  • Partly mean pooling, partly not in the RNA. The states of 13 NK/T gene tokens keep more than the gene-mean of the same forward pass (layer 12, MLP: 0.58 / 0.62 / 0.72 / 0.56 vs 0.41 / 0.43 / 0.49 / 0.45), about as much already at the input layer: it is mostly which genes are detected and at what rank, and TEDDY’s layers neither add nor remove much of it. Even the best probe keeps only about half to three-quarters, and CD3E is among the tokens in only 45% of NK and 60% of T pair cells. So a non-linear, token-aware readout of frozen TEDDY could recover part of the NK–T difference. Caveats: one site; probes, not the head’s objective; token probes fit on 990 random site4 NK/T cells and scored on the 483 pair cells; some intervals overlap.

Corrections and disclosures (29 Sep 2026): Experiments 1, 3, 4, 5 rerun; 2 and 6 withdrawn
  • Marker order: fixed. ANM’s engine entered the markers one per step in panel order, a hidden weight. In the rerun all markers enter at once, and the answer key, the fixed rule and ANM share one scoring function. ANM’s scores are then a fixed multiple of the rule’s, so ANM makes the same call as TEDDY + fixed rule (re-coded for each question) on every site4 cell, for all three questions. What ANM adds is a declared, auditable layer: the question is written down, the checks come with it, and every call has a per-marker reason.
  • Per-cell scaling: fixed. The head’s predictions were scaled by a per-cell factor computed from the cell’s measured protein, the answer key. The rerun uses one train-median factor, 0.92663 (67,405 training cells).
  • Which change moved which number. The fixed rule’s “no calls” under the soft and B/T-priority rules (101 → 448, 1,796 → 3,169) moved because of the new size factor alone; the shared scoring leaves them unchanged. The strict rule is redefined (a weighted mean with key markers at weight 2): its “no calls” go 881 → 1,277 from the scoring and 1,277 → 2,014 from the size factor. The official TEDDY-G preprocessing then gives 272 / 1,493 / 2,551.
  • The old ANM accuracy edge came only from declining more. Old run, B/T-priority rule: ANM 0.960 vs rule 0.943 on calls made, but at ANM’s coverage (3,373 declined) the old rule scored 0.966.
  • Experiment 3: CD5 decided 23.6% of calls in the first run; now CD2 decides most often (17.9%), CD5 close behind (17.3%); the shuffle now keeps values within their lineage (p ≈ 0.0099, the smallest 100 shuffles can give). With all markers at once the deciding marker is the protein with the largest normalised value in the called lineage, so it is not specific to ANM.
  • Experiment 4: ANM’s confidence (soft_P) never gates better than the rule’s own margin at the same coverage (every difference ≤ 0; B/T-priority, 40%: 0.9988 vs 0.9988). soft_P reaches 1.11, so it is a score, not a probability. The hard cells are mostly out of scope: NK/ILC 3.1× and erythroid 3.4× over-represented.
  • Experiment 5: labels are now drawn from a pool disjoint from the scored cells (11,725 / 5,025). Labels to match ANM: logistic 500, MLP 1,000 (was 200), threshold grid 50.
  • Answer key. It still has three classes (B, T, myeloid) and no NK or out-of-scope class. Out-of-scope cells (NK, ILC, erythroid, progenitor) still get a call 73–95% of the time, from ANM and the rule alike.
  • Experiment 2 (RNA missing): withdrawn pending redesign. Prototype: with degraded RNA fed to TEDDY, ANM with a declared trust per level makes the same decisions as the fixed rule with a stricter bar per level.
  • Experiment 6 (look-alike cells): withdrawn pending redesign. Prototype: the TEDDY + head predictions do separate NK from T in the right direction but compress the differences to about a quarter to a third of the measured ones (CD335 0.88). On site4, 1053 of the 1,506 NK–T look-alike pairs involve “gdT CD158b+” cells, which look NK-like in both predicted and measured protein (an annotation problem, not a pipeline failure); on the other 453 pairs the median within-pair gap in predicted (TEDDY + head) CD56 / CD94 / CD335 / CD3 is 0.148 / 0.100 / 0.364 / 0.205 vs measured 0.621 / 0.321 / 0.413 / 0.756.
  • Phase 1 re-evaluated with the train-median factor: test Pearson 0.610 → 0.603 (MLP) and 0.596 → 0.588 (flow matching); panel proteins drop by at most 0.013. With the official preprocessing: 0.603 (MLP) and 0.582 (flow matching), T-cell panel markers slightly lower.
  • Phase 2 retrained on the official embedding after its fixes (ADT transformed once, train-median size factor). Test Pearson (134 proteins), RNA-only / protein-only / joint: 0.613 / 0.767 / 0.763 decoded directly, 0.211 / 0.216 / 0.219 from one flow-matching sample, 0.358 / 0.382 / 0.379 from the mean of 5; the legacy arm (old double transform, measured size factor) decodes to 0.622 / 0.745 / 0.738. So the old ≈ 0.25 came mostly from scoring one flow-matching sample; the fix adds about 0.02 when protein is an input; RNA-only is 0.613 against the legacy arm’s 0.622, which used each cell’s own measured protein depth (the leak), and is now slightly above phase 1 (0.603). Protein-only and joint inputs contain the measured protein: reconstructions, not predictions.
  • TEDDY preprocessing: now official. The earlier embedding was a mean over real tokens at 512 tokens with no gene-median normalisation. Every number on this page now comes from TEDDY-G’s official preprocessing (counts ÷ total × 10⁴, ÷ TEDDY’s non-zero gene medians, top-2,048 rank tokens, no CLS token, mean over gene tokens); phase-1 Pearson is unchanged (0.603 vs 0.603).
  • “0 labels for a new question” holds for any written rule, including the fixed rule; it is not specific to ANM.
  • Cells. Donor 15078 is in training at other sites and is 32.6% of the site4 test cells. The “out-of-site check” is held-out donor 18303 at a training site, which was also the validation set. The 9 panel proteins were chosen by test-set Pearson.

00 — The setup

How ANM connects to TEDDY

Three roles, one direction: TEDDY + our head turn RNA into evidence, ANM decides, the measured proteins grade. Nothing flows back into TEDDY.

Evidence source · frozen

TEDDY + our head

  • Frozen TEDDY reads only RNA → a 512-number embedding; never retrained or opened.
  • Our head, trained once on training sites’ proteins, predicts 134 (Pearson ≈ 0.60); 9 picked by test-set Pearson.
  • Embedding from TEDDY-G’s official preprocessing; the earlier non-official one gave the same Pearson.
Decision layer · added, not trained

ANM

  • Reads 9 predicted proteins as typed evidence (exceptions: table below).
  • A written question (the observer) → a call or “no call”: the fixed rule’s call for that question.
  • No learned weights; open loop, so its dynamics are not tested here.
Answer key · held out

Measured proteins

  • CITE-seq measures the same cell’s proteins, kept aside.
  • They grade calls with the rule’s and ANM’s scoring function.
  • 3 classes, no NK class: 1,342 of 1,690 NK cells count as “myeloid” (soft rule).

Two separate axes: phases build the evidence, modes say how ANM uses TEDDY

02 — How we compare

One fair test, reused in every experiment

“ANM helps TEDDY” means TEDDY + ANM beats TEDDY + fixed rule on a decision property a trained head needs labels for, TEDDY and our head unchanged. After the rerun no experiment shows better calls; what remains is a declared layer: written question, checks, per-marker reasons.

Four rules

R1
Same frozen TEDDY and headNeither is retrained (best.pt is the head). Rule and ANM read the same 9 predicted proteins as typed evidence; classifiers in Experiments 1 and 5 add 32 embedding numbers.
R2
Same cells16,750 held-out cells from site4 plus an “out-of-site check” on 2,000; donor 15078 (32.6% of site4) is in training at other sites; donor 18303 was the validation set.
R3
Truth only checks answersMeasured proteins score the calls, with labelled exceptions (withdrawn Experiments 2 and 6); one train-median size factor, 0.92663, not a per-cell one.
R4
Decision metrics firstDeclining, label cost, per-marker reasons, coverage vs accuracy, false agreement. Accuracy is reported, never the win condition.

Three methods

Baseline

TEDDY + fixed rule

Predicted proteins → a score per lineage → the best if it clears the bar (the soft rule).

Treatment

TEDDY + ANM

The same predictions → ANM’s engine under a written observer → a call or “no call”, with workability and exactness checks.

Control

TEDDY + trained classifier

A classifier fitted on labelled cells (inputs vary by experiment), modelled on Jev’s interface; Jev was never called.

help = (TEDDY + ANM) − (TEDDY + fixed rule) on the same predictions,  with TEDDY + trained classifier as the “just train harder” control

So a difference comes from the decision layer: both read the same evidence (Experiment 6 changes the evidence itself); the classifiers’ extra 32 embedding numbers can only favour them.

“Dynamics not tested” is not “no time here”: ANM’s step is an order, not necessarily a clock

        
ANM’s step t is any declared processing order, not necessarily physical time, as long as the order is real.

        
        

          

            Not a real order
            Panel order of the nine markers
            
CD19CD72CD22CD3CD2CD5CD16CD11cCD36

            
They come from one prediction, so feeding them in panel order made memory decay a hidden weight, fixed by entering all at once.

          

          

            A real order
            TEDDY’s transformer layers
            
hl+1 = hl + fl(hl)⇒ δhl+1 = (I + ∂fl/∂h) δhl

            
The residual stream is the retained state, and attention couples the gene tokens.

          

        

        
So field dynamics on TEDDY are not yet tested, not impossible: test them in Mode A over layers, pre-registered; in the ANM–Jev multimodal tests the field gained only when arrival order carried information (+1.4 points), and a 2-step window beat it by 7 points.
